Welcome! I am a PhD candidate in [New York University's Wilf Family Department of Politics](https://as.nyu.edu/departments/politics.html), specializing in international political economy and comparative politics.

I study the political consequences of major economic transformations, such as those caused by globalization. I am particularly interested in three questions: how, and under what conditions, economic shocks contribute to major political change; how they shape the formation of political coalitions; and how these coalitions influence policy in the long run.

My dissertation, supervised by Shanker Satyanath (chair), Pablo Querubín, and Zuhad Hai, examines these questions in three periods of acceleration and reversal of globalization in Latin America: the first wave of globalization, the interwar deglobalization, and the trade liberalization of the 1990s. My job market paper, "Trading Away the Machine," addresses the first of these questions by studying how trade liberalization affects support for incumbents in the developing world. My research has been supported by NYU's Dean's Dissertation Fellowship and by an external fellowship from the Institute for Humane Studies.

Before joining NYU, I received an MA in Social Sciences from the [Juan March-Carlos III Institute](https://ic3jm.es/en/), and a BA in Economics from [Centro de Investigación y Docencia Económicas (CIDE)](https://www.cide.edu/).
